Module 2

MVM: Concept & Rationale

Why is the best estimate of liabilities not enough? This module explains the transfer-value concept, the role of non-hedgeable risks, and why a rational third party demands more than the BEL to assume insurance liabilities.

The Limits of the Best Estimate

The Best Estimate of Liabilities (BEL) is the probability-weighted present value of all future insurance cash flows (claims, expenses, premiums) discounted at the risk-free interest rate. It is the actuarial "expected value" of the liability portfolio.

While the BEL is theoretically correct in an expected-value sense, it has a fundamental limitation: insurance liabilities are not tradeable on a deep liquid market. There is no exchange where you can buy or sell a portfolio of life insurance policies at the press of a button.

The Illiquidity Problem

For a traded security (e.g., a bond), the market price already incorporates all information about risk — including the compensation investors demand for bearing that risk. The BEL, by contrast, only captures the expected cash flows. It ignores the fact that actually managing those liabilities requires capital, which has a cost.

The Transfer Value Concept

The SST — like Solvency II — defines the market-consistent value of liabilities as the amount for which they could be transferred to a knowledgeable, willing third party in an arm's-length transaction. This is the transfer value.

Imagine that an insurer becomes financially distressed. FINMA would need to find another insurer (the "reference undertaking") willing to take over all the policyholders and continue honoring their contracts. The key question is:

Central Question: What is the minimum amount the reference undertaking would require — on top of the best estimate — to agree to assume the liability portfolio?

That additional amount is the Market Value Margin (MVM). It compensates the reference undertaking for the cost of holding regulatory capital against the non-hedgeable risks embedded in the liability portfolio throughout its entire run-off period.

Transfer Value Identity
Transfer Value = BEL + MVM
This is the minimum amount a rational, well-capitalised insurer would accept to assume the liability portfolio at arm's length.

Hedgeable vs Non-Hedgeable Risks

A critical distinction in the MVM framework is between risks that can be hedged in capital markets and risks that cannot.

Risk type Examples Hedgeable? In MVM?
Market risk Interest rate risk, equity risk, FX risk Yes No
Life underwriting Mortality, longevity, lapse, disability, expenses No Yes
Non-life underwriting Premium risk, reserve risk, catastrophe risk No Yes
Health underwriting Morbidity risk, medical cost inflation No Yes
Counterparty credit Reinsurance counterparty default No (partially) Yes
Operational risk Systems failures, legal risk, fraud No No (excluded)

The reference undertaking is assumed to hold only risk-free assets to back the liability portfolio. Because it has perfectly matched the asset-side risk with risk-free instruments, market risk is fully hedged and does not require additional capital. Only the non-hedgeable underwriting and credit risks remain, and it is for these that the MVM compensates.

The Reference Undertaking

The reference undertaking is a hypothetical insurer with the following characteristics:

  • It takes over exactly the same liability portfolio (same policies, same cash flows).
  • It holds only risk-free assets to back the liabilities (no market risk).
  • It is subject to the same regulatory capital requirements (SST rules apply to it too).
  • It is well-capitalised — it can actually meet the capital requirements.
  • It operates at the minimum required capital level at each point in time.
Simplifying assumption: The reference undertaking operates with no operational risk, no market risk, and no diversification benefits from other business lines. This means the MVM is computed on a "stand-alone" basis for the liability portfolio.

From Capital Requirement to MVM

Since the reference undertaking is subject to SST rules, it must hold regulatory capital against the non-hedgeable risks at every point during the run-off. Holding this capital has a cost — the capital could otherwise be returned to shareholders or invested elsewhere.

The MVM compensates the reference undertaking for this opportunity cost. It equals the present value of all future capital charges:

MVM — Conceptual Formula
MVM = PV of (CoC rate × Capital requirement at each future date)
CoC rate = Cost of Capital rate (6% p.a. in SST)
Capital requirement at each future date = SST capital for non-hedgeable risks if the portfolio were re-valued at that point in time.

A Simple Illustration

Illustration: Life Insurance Run-off

Consider a life insurer with a 20-year annuity portfolio. At valuation date:

  • BEL = CHF 1,000 million (present value of expected annuity payments)
  • The capital required for longevity risk (a non-hedgeable risk) is CHF 50m today.
  • Over 20 years, as the portfolio runs off, this capital requirement shrinks.
  • At 6% CoC, the cost of holding capital each year totals, say, CHF 18 million in present value.
  • Therefore MVM ≈ CHF 18 million, and MCVL = CHF 1,018 million.

The MVM of CHF 18m is the minimum extra amount a reference undertaking would demand to take over this annuity book. Without it, they would be bearing capital costs out of their own pocket.

What the MVM Is Not

  • MVM is not a risk premium on the probability of exceeding the best estimate.
  • MVM is not a safety margin added to be prudent.
  • MVM is not a profit margin. The reference undertaking earns exactly its cost of capital.
Key Takeaways — Module 2
  • Insurance liabilities are not traded on liquid markets, so the BEL alone understates the true transfer value.
  • The MVM = the present value of the cost of holding regulatory capital for non-hedgeable risks during the run-off.
  • The reference undertaking holds only risk-free assets and is subject to SST capital requirements.
  • Only non-hedgeable risks enter the MVM: life, non-life, and health underwriting risk, and reinsurance credit risk.
  • Market risk is excluded because the reference undertaking can hedge it by holding risk-free assets.
  • MVM is an economic concept, not an arbitrary safety margin or risk premium.
