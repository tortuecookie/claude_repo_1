Market Value Margin
& the Swiss Solvency Test

A comprehensive course on how Switzerland quantifies the cost of transferring insurance liabilities — from regulatory foundations to numerical examples.

About This Course

The Market Value Margin (MVM) is one of the most technically demanding concepts in the Swiss Solvency Test (SST). It represents the additional amount above the best estimate of liabilities that a reference undertaking would require to take on those liabilities — in other words, the cost of capital for running off non-hedgeable insurance risks.

This course walks through the SST framework, the rationale and methodology for the MVM, practical calculation techniques, and comparisons with the analogous Solvency II concept (the Risk Margin).

Learning Objectives

●

Understand the structure and purpose of the Swiss Solvency Test

●

Explain why a Market Value Margin exists above the best estimate

●

Apply the cost-of-capital formula to compute the MVM step by step

●

Identify which risk modules enter the MVM calculation and why

●

Work through numerical examples for life and non-life portfolios

●

Compare MVM under SST with the Solvency II Risk Margin

Who Is This Course For?

This course is designed for actuaries, risk managers, and finance professionals working with Swiss insurers, as well as students preparing for actuarial exams or roles in insurance regulation. A basic familiarity with insurance concepts (liabilities, discounting, probability of ruin) is helpful, but the early modules introduce all necessary background.

How to use this course: Work through modules in order, as each builds on the previous one. Module 5 contains worked numerical examples — you will get the most from it after completing Modules 1–4. Module 6 can also be read independently as a reference.
