Advanced Topics & Comparisons

An in-depth comparison of MVM (SST) and Risk Margin (Solvency II), criticisms of the CoC approach, internal model considerations, and FINMA reporting requirements.

The Operational Risk Difference

Solvency II includes operational risk in the SCR for the Risk Margin; SST excludes it from SCRMVM. The rationale for the SST exclusion is that operational risk is largely independent of the liability run-off profile and is difficult to project meaningfully over a multi-year horizon.

This means SST MVMs tend to be slightly lower than Solvency II Risk Margins for otherwise identical portfolios.

Criticisms of the Cost-of-Capital Approach

Major Criticisms
  • The 6% rate is arbitrary. Calibrated in a higher-rate environment; overstated when risk-free rates are low.
  • Pro-cyclicality. When risk-free rates fall (stress periods), MVM increases, worsening SST ratios at the worst time.
  • No liquidity premium. Real insurers earn illiquidity premia; ignoring this overstates the cost of running off liabilities.
  • Proportional simplifications can be misleading when SCR/BEL ratio is not constant over time.
  • Double-counting concern. The MVM in TC may partly double-count risk already captured in the ES component.

Internal Models and MVM

Under the SST, insurers may use internal models rather than the FINMA standard model, subject to FINMA approval. This affects MVM by producing potentially different (often lower) SCRMVM(0) values.

Practical note: Even companies with approved internal models for the ES component sometimes use the proportional simplification for MVM projection, as the incremental accuracy may not materially change the MVM outcome.

Recent Developments

Solvency II Review (EU) — Implications for SST

EIOPA's 2021–2022 review of Solvency II introduced a lambda factor (λ) that reduces the Risk Margin in proportion to the risk-free rate level, addressing the concern that 6% CoC overstates the true cost in low-rate environments (effective 2026).

FINMA had not introduced a comparable adjustment to the SST MVM as of the time of writing. Swiss insurers and the Swiss Actuarial Association (SAA) have called for a similar review.

IASB IFRS 17 and the Risk Adjustment

Under IFRS 17 (effective 2023), insurers report a Risk Adjustment (RA) for non-financial risk in their accounts. The RA is conceptually similar to the MVM but:

  • Not prescribed as a CoC method — companies can use CoC, quantile, or other approaches.
  • IFRS 17 requires disclosure of the confidence level equivalent of the RA.
  • The SST MVM remains a regulatory (not accounting) concept computed separately.
IFRS 17 vs SST: Do not confuse the IFRS 17 Risk Adjustment with the SST Market Value Margin. They serve different purposes, follow different rules, and will generally produce different values for the same portfolio.

MVM in the Broader SST Ecosystem

SST Ratio — Expanded View
SST Ratio = [A − BEL − MVM] / [ES99%(−ΔRBC) + MVM]
↑ MVM → lower RBC (numerator falls) AND higher TC (denominator rises)
A larger MVM has a doubly negative effect on the SST ratio.
Key Takeaways — Module 6
  • MVM (SST) and Risk Margin (Solvency II) use the same 6% CoC formula but differ on the base risk measure and inclusion of operational risk.
  • The 6% CoC rate is criticized for being pro-cyclical; Solvency II introduced a lambda adjustment in 2026.
  • Internal model users may calculate SCRMVM using their own calibrations, subject to FINMA approval.
  • MVM appears in both numerator and denominator of the SST ratio, giving it a doubly negative effect when it increases.
  • IFRS 17 Risk Adjustment and the SST MVM are distinct concepts measured under different frameworks.
  • Annual SST reports are submitted to FINMA by 30 April; group SST ratios are publicly disclosed.
