Module 3

The Cost of Capital Approach

The MVM is calculated using the cost-of-capital method. This module covers the 6% CoC rate, why market risk is excluded, how future capital requirements are projected, and how they are discounted to a present value.

What Is the Cost of Capital?

Investors who provide capital to an insurer (e.g., by holding equity) expect a return commensurate with the risk they take. The cost of capital (CoC) is the minimum rate of return that equity investors require to invest in a given firm.

For the MVM calculation, the CoC rate represents the excess return above the risk-free rate that the reference undertaking's shareholders require. In other words, if the risk-free rate is r, the reference undertaking needs to earn r + CoC on its regulatory capital.

Since the reference undertaking invests only in risk-free assets (earning exactly r), it earns nothing beyond the risk-free return from its investment portfolio. The MVM must cover the CoC shortfall.

Economic Intuition

Suppose the reference undertaking holds CHF 100 of regulatory capital at time t. It earns the risk-free rate r on that capital. Its shareholders require r + 6%. The 6% gap — CHF 6 per year on each CHF 100 of capital — must be recovered from the MVM. The MVM is simply the present value of all these annual CHF 6 charges over the entire run-off horizon.

The 6% CoC Rate

Under the SST, the cost-of-capital rate is set at 6% per annum. This is the same rate used under Solvency II (EU) for the analogous Risk Margin calculation.

Which Capital Requirement? The SCRMVM

To compute the MVM, we need the capital requirement for non-hedgeable risks at each future point in time. We call this the SCRMVM.

  • Include: life underwriting risk, non-life underwriting risk, health underwriting risk, credit risk on reinsurance.
  • Exclude: market risk, operational risk, business risk.
Why exclude market risk? The reference undertaking perfectly matches its assets to risk-free instruments — no asset–liability mismatch and no exposure to interest rate, equity, or credit spread movements.

Projecting Future Capital Requirements

For each future year, the capital requirement must be projected as the portfolio runs off. Key drivers:

  • Policy count reduction: portfolio shrinks as policyholders exit.
  • Duration: long-tail lines decay slowly; short-tail lines fall quickly.
  • Claim settlement: as claims are paid, reserve risk decreases.
  • No new business: the reference undertaking runs off existing liabilities only.
Run-off Profile: Conceptual Example

A non-life reserve risk portfolio with CHF 500m of outstanding claims:

  • Year 0: SCRMVM = CHF 80m
  • Year 1: SCRMVM = CHF 62m
  • Year 3: SCRMVM = CHF 35m
  • Year 7: SCRMVM = CHF 9m
  • Year 10+: SCRMVM ≈ 0

Discounting

Annual capital charges must be discounted to present value using the risk-free term structure.

Discounting the Capital Charge at Year t
PV of charge = CoC × SCRMVM(t) / (1 + rt+1)t+1
rt+1 = risk-free spot rate for maturity (t+1) years from valuation date
MVM — Full Formula
MVM = CoC × Σt=0T−1 SCRMVM(t) / (1 + rt+1)t+1
CoC = 6% p.a.  |  T = run-off horizon  |  rt+1 = risk-free spot rate

Key Takeaways — Module 3
  • The CoC rate of 6% p.a. is the excess return above risk-free that reference undertaking shareholders require.
  • 6% is a regulatory constant prescribed by FINMA — it does not vary by firm or market conditions.
  • SCRMVM includes underwriting risk and reinsurance credit risk, but excludes market risk and operational risk.
  • Market risk is excluded because the reference undertaking holds risk-free assets.
  • Future SCRMVM values must be projected and discounted to present value using the risk-free term structure.
  • MVM is increasing in CoC rate, liability duration, and SCRMVM; decreasing in risk-free rates.
