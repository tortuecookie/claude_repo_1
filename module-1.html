Module 1

The Swiss Solvency Test

An overview of Switzerland's economic solvency framework — its history, structure, and the key quantities that underpin capital adequacy for Swiss insurers.

Background and Regulatory Context

The Swiss Solvency Test (SST) is Switzerland's risk-based capital adequacy framework for insurance companies. It is administered by FINMA (Eidgenössische Finanzmarktaufsicht, the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority) and has been mandatory for all Swiss insurers and reinsurers since 1 January 2011.

Development of the SST began in 2003, making Switzerland one of the earliest countries to adopt a fully economic, market-consistent solvency framework. The SST was designed to overcome the shortcomings of earlier rule-based systems, which relied on book values and fixed factors rather than the actual economic exposure of an insurer.

Core Principle

The SST is built on economic and market-consistent valuation. All assets and liabilities are valued at the amounts a rational, well-informed market participant would agree to in an arm's-length transaction. Capital requirements reflect the actual risk profile of the insurer, not regulatory artefacts.

The SST Balance Sheet

Under the SST, the balance sheet is restated on a market-consistent basis. This differs substantially from the statutory (accounting) balance sheet.

Item Description Valuation basis
Market-consistent assets (A) All invested assets, reinsurance recoverables, other assets Fair (market) value
Best Estimate Liabilities (BEL) Expected present value of future insurance cash flows Risk-free discounting
Market Value Margin (MVM) Cost of capital for non-hedgeable risks over the run-off period Cost-of-capital method
Other liabilities Subordinated debt, financial liabilities Fair value

The total market-consistent value of insurance liabilities is therefore:

Market-Consistent Value of Liabilities
MCVL = BEL + MVM
BEL  = Best Estimate Liabilities (probability-weighted PV of future cash flows)
MVM = Market Value Margin (see Modules 2–4)

Risk-Bearing Capital (RBC)

The Risk-Bearing Capital (RBC) is the SST equivalent of "available capital" or "own funds". It measures the financial cushion available to absorb unexpected losses:

Risk-Bearing Capital
RBC = A − BEL − MVM − Other liabilities
In simplified notation (ignoring other liabilities):
RBC = Market-consistent assets − MCVL

RBC is analogous to the "excess of assets over liabilities" but measured on a fully market-consistent basis. A positive RBC means the insurer holds more market-consistent assets than liabilities, providing a buffer for adverse scenarios.

Target Capital (TC)

The Target Capital (TC) represents the minimum level of RBC that FINMA requires an insurer to hold. It captures the risk that RBC could deteriorate over a one-year horizon due to market movements, insurance losses, or other adverse events.

Formally, TC is based on the Expected Shortfall (ES) at the 99% confidence level of the random variable representing the one-year change in RBC. Expected Shortfall (also called Conditional Value at Risk, CVaR) is a coherent risk measure that captures the average loss in the worst 1% of scenarios:

Target Capital
TC = ES99%(−ΔRBC) + MVM
−ΔRBC  = decrease in RBC over one year (a loss)
ES99% = Expected Shortfall at 99% confidence (average loss in worst 1% of scenarios)
MVM         = Market Value Margin (ensures liabilities can be transferred)
Why add MVM to TC? The ES captures unexpected deterioration over one year. The MVM ensures that even after a stress event, the insurer holds enough capital to transfer its liabilities to a third party at their full market-consistent value.

The SST Ratio

The SST solvency condition is expressed as a ratio. An insurer is solvent under the SST if and only if:

SST Solvency Condition
SST Ratio = RBC / TC ≥ 100%
An SST ratio below 100% requires the insurer to submit a remediation plan to FINMA.
Ratios are reported annually and disclosed publicly at the group level.

Risk Categories in the SST

The TC (specifically, the ES component) is driven by the following risk categories:

Risk category Key risk drivers Typical method
Market risk Interest rates, equities, FX, real estate, spreads Variance–covariance or scenario-based
Insurance risk Mortality, longevity, lapse, catastrophe, reserve risk Standard model or internal model
Credit risk Reinsurance counterparty default, bond issuer default Factor-based or rating-based
Scenario risk Pandemic, earthquake, financial crisis, etc. Pre-defined FINMA scenarios

These risk categories interact through a correlation matrix. The total TC is typically less than the sum of individual risk capitals thanks to diversification effects.

SST vs Solvency II: A Quick Comparison

Feature SST (Switzerland) Solvency II (EU)
Jurisdiction Switzerland (FINMA) European Union (EIOPA)
Risk measure Expected Shortfall (ES) at 99% Value-at-Risk (VaR) at 99.5%
Time horizon 1 year 1 year
Liability valuation BEL + MVM Best Estimate + Risk Margin
Cost-of-capital rate 6% 6%
Mandatory internal models No (standard model available) No (standard formula available)
Effective since 2011 2016
Note on ES vs VaR: An Expected Shortfall at 99% is generally more conservative than a Value-at-Risk at 99.5% for fat-tailed distributions, but less conservative for thin-tailed ones. In practice, SST and Solvency II capital requirements tend to be broadly comparable in magnitude.
Key Takeaways — Module 1
  • The SST is Switzerland's economic solvency framework, mandatory since 2011 and administered by FINMA.
  • All assets and liabilities are valued on a market-consistent basis.
  • Insurance liabilities = BEL + MVM. The MVM is the focus of this course.
  • Risk-Bearing Capital (RBC) is the available capital; Target Capital (TC) is the required capital.
  • The SST ratio = RBC / TC must be ≥ 100% for an insurer to be considered solvent.
  • TC is based on the 99% Expected Shortfall of the one-year change in RBC, plus the MVM.
